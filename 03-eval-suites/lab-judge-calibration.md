## Diagnosis

The judge is lenient. All 4 disagreements are false passes (T-02, T-03, T-07, T-09), and it never failed an answer I passed. There are two patterns:

- **Fluent, confident padding (T-03, T-09).** The answers read well, but the figures they cite are not in the retrieved source. The judge rewarded length and confidence over grounding.
- **Partial or contradicted grounding (T-02, T-07).** T-07 lists five integrations and one ("Salesforce") is not in the workspace config. T-02 says "Yes, it renews annually" while its own note says no renewal clause appears. The judge let both through because the rest of the content was supported.

The rubric already says an answer must be grounded in the retrieved source AND answer the question, so the judge is misapplying it, not missing a rule. The revision below makes the grounding rule explicit.

## Rubric revision

1. **Explicit rule:** *Any claim, figure, or list item not traceable to the retrieved source = FAIL, regardless of length, fluency, or confidence. One ungrounded item fails the whole answer.*
2. **One-shot fail example (not from the 12 traces, to avoid overfitting):** a confident, well-written explanation of a tiered termination fee, where the retrieved source contains no termination fee. The answer is fluent and specific but ungrounded, so it is a FAIL.
3. **Scope note:** this rubric scores answer quality only. An honest "not found in the retrieved records" (T-06, T-10) answers no question, so it scores FAIL here by design, and I kept those labels as FAIL so the same rubric applies to every trace. Whether an abstention is correct behavior is evaluated separately under Robustness (M1), not by this judge.
4. **Re-measure (pending):** re-run the 12 traces with the revised rubric and recompute κ against my labels.
